AI-assisted search results

Customers expect search to just work. Customer complaints were increasing as well as the number of unique searches. Our traditional keyword-based search techniques were no longer cutting it. With generative AI, we are now able to understand customer intent no matter what they search. This effort aimed to enhance relevancy and discovery in search results by experimenting with emerging technologies.

Design lead

Shaping the direction

I was the primary designer on an exploratory initiative to learn how generative AI could work with search by building a proof of concept. I explored different approaches and proposed the design we built for the POC. My concepts were used to facilitate discussions and build a POV around how AI appears in our CX. Should it be the new search experience or should it be separated? How do we align it to customer needs while also helping them discover new ways they can search? How do we calibrate the amount of trust they put into it? How do we incrementally release and learn before scaling to all customers?

Customer reaction was positive and highlighted improvements

  • Customers we spoke with had a broadly positive perception of H-E-B using AI to help them search and expressed excitement to using it in their shopping experience.

  • Customers have been trained to search for specific things when using our site and will need guidance to know they can try more complex queries.

  • Customers expected inferred personalization based on past purchases and browsing patterns. They assume the system already knows them.

Overall, this brief test gave us confidence that leveraging AI to meet customers where they already are and enhance their search experience in subtle ways would be well received.

As we began building for scale, we discovered several areas where the results were performing poorly

Even though we were early in the build process, we organized an internal alpha test to help us understand how far away from shippable we are.

I shaped the LLM’s behavior and significantly improved results

After the alpha, our backend engineers moved to other initiatives. Prompt optimization was the only thing we could still invest in, but our lead engineer didn’t have time to take it on, so I volunteered to play the role of prompt engineer.

I spent time with our lead backend engineer to get my local system set up, then used the prompt optimization codebase to iterate on the prompt, test it and evaluate its behavior.

Testing the improvements validated the significant improvement we saw in the eval

We ran another internal test after prompt optimization, and the responses validated our improvements. Here’s what people said.

  • “Huge improvement from phase I with more variety and applicable products.”

  • “More intentional and aligned to search prompt. Curated and effective geared towards a solution”

  • “If I didn’t know exactly what I was looking for, looking for inspiration, or hoping for a more convenient shop I would choose the AI tool.”

  • “Still feeling like the top of the page doesn’t tell you the story of what was returned for you. Needs a blurb, ‘Pick your favorite ingredients and bundle them together to make a fantastic meal!’”
